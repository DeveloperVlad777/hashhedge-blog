But during a prop trading Challenge, the way you choose what to trade needs to change.
It's not just about potential profit. You also need to consider liquidity, volatility, spreads, and the risk of sudden price movements against your position.

Why Your Choice of Asset Matters More Than You Think
When trading your own account, you decide how much risk you're willing to take.
A highly volatile altcoin might offer a bigger potential return, but it also comes with a greater risk of sharp moves against your position.
During a prop trading Challenge, you also have to stay within the Daily Loss Limit and Total Loss Limit.
That means a single losing trade on a volatile asset can significantly reduce your remaining loss-limit buffer.
This becomes especially important when using leverage. Even a relatively small move in the underlying asset can cause a substantial change in your position's PnL.
At Hash Hedge, there's no consistency rule, either during the Challenge or on a Funded Account.
You don't need to spread your profits across multiple trades or trading days to meet additional requirements.
The main restrictions are your account's loss limits.
So instead of asking, "Which asset could make me the most money?", a better question is:
"Which asset gives me clear setups that fit my trading strategy and risk tolerance?"
Bitcoin: The Most Liquid Market in Crypto
Bitcoin is the largest cryptocurrency and one of the most liquid markets in the industry.
For traders, that comes with several practical advantages.
High Liquidity
High trading volume generally makes it easier to open and close positions without significantly affecting the market price.
This is especially important when trading larger positions or during periods of increased market activity.
Tight Spreads
BTC typically has tighter bid-ask spreads than less liquid altcoins.
For active traders, this can reduce the cost of entering and exiting positions.
Market Structure
Bitcoin's high liquidity makes it a useful market for analyzing swing highs and lows, liquidity zones, order blocks, Fair Value Gaps (FVGs), and other technical structures.
However, that doesn't mean BTC's technical levels are automatically "institutional" or guaranteed to hold.
They're still areas of interest that need to be evaluated within the current market structure.
Clear Trading Session Activity
Bitcoin traders can monitor changes in liquidity and volatility throughout the Asian, London, and New York trading sessions.
For example, a trader might watch for price to break out of the Asian session range after London or New York opens.
But these patterns don't follow a fixed schedule and shouldn't be treated as entry signals on their own.
Less Exposure to Low-Liquidity Price Spikes
Bitcoin can still experience liquidation cascades and major price swings, especially during important news events.
However, its high liquidity generally makes it less sensitive to individual large orders than many smaller altcoins.
None of these advantages make BTC automatically more predictable or profitable.
For prop traders, Bitcoin's main strengths are high liquidity, deep order books, relatively tight spreads, and substantial trading volume.
Ethereum: An Alternative to Bitcoin
Ethereum is also one of the most liquid cryptocurrencies and shares many characteristics with Bitcoin.
However, ETH has a few important differences worth considering.
Higher Volatility
During certain periods, ETH can be more volatile than Bitcoin.
This creates opportunities for larger profits on individual trades, but it also increases the potential for losses.
That's why using the same position size for BTC and ETH doesn't necessarily mean taking the same amount of risk.
Position size should be calculated based on your Stop Loss distance and predefined risk per trade.
Ethereum-Specific Catalysts
Beyond broader market and macroeconomic events, Ethereum can be affected by network upgrades, staking-related developments, Layer 2 growth, and other ecosystem events.
These factors can temporarily cause ETH to move differently from Bitcoin.
Traders should monitor both the economic calendar and major developments within the Ethereum ecosystem.
An Established Derivatives Market
Ethereum has an active derivatives market, with data available on futures, open interest, funding rates, and liquidations.
These indicators can provide additional context for market analysis, but they don't reliably predict where price will move next.
For traders completing a prop Challenge, ETH can serve as an additional market alongside BTC.
But there's no need to add Ethereum simply to increase the number of trades you take.
If your strategy already produces enough setups on Bitcoin, trading more assets won't automatically improve your results.
Altcoins: Opportunities and Risks
Altcoins vary widely in market capitalization, liquidity, volatility, and market depth.
That's why treating every cryptocurrency outside BTC and ETH as part of one trading category can be misleading.
Large-cap altcoins may have liquid markets and relatively tight spreads.
Less popular assets, on the other hand, can move several percentage points in response to relatively small orders.
When Large-Cap Altcoins May Be Worth Trading
Some altcoins, including Solana (SOL), BNB, and other large-cap assets, have well-developed spot and derivatives markets.
You can apply many of the same technical analysis concepts used for BTC and ETH:
✓
Market structure
✓
Order blocks
✓
Fair Value Gaps (FVGs)
✓
Liquidity sweeps
✓
Premium and discount zones
However, altcoins often experience higher volatility.
That can create larger price movements, but it also increases the risk of sudden moves against your position.
Higher potential returns don't automatically make an altcoin a better choice for a prop Challenge.
Key Risks of Trading Altcoins
Token Unlocks
Many crypto projects have scheduled token unlocks for team members, investors, and other stakeholders.
These events can increase the circulating supply and affect market prices.
Checking the token unlock calendar before opening a position can help you account for this risk.
Low Market Depth
An altcoin chart might show a clear technical setup, but there may be significantly less liquidity at individual price levels compared to BTC or ETH.
A large order can move through these levels quickly and trigger sharp price changes.
Project-Specific News
Altcoins react not only to broader market conditions and macroeconomic news but also to protocol upgrades, exchange listings, token unlocks, regulatory developments, and other project-specific events.
That means checking the economic calendar alone may not be enough to manage news-related risks.
Wider Spreads and Slippage
Lower liquidity can lead to higher trading costs.
Wider spreads make entries and exits more expensive, while sudden price movements increase the risk of slippage.
During a prop Challenge, these costs matter because they affect your PnL and reduce your remaining loss-limit buffer.
What to Consider When Trading Less Liquid Altcoins
The smaller and less liquid an asset is, the more carefully you need to evaluate its trading conditions.
Smaller altcoins may have:
✓
Wider bid-ask spreads
✓
Lower order book depth
✓
Greater sensitivity to individual large orders
✓
Higher exposure to project-specific news
✓
Sharper price movements and increased slippage
That's why a fixed rule like "never trade anything outside the top 20 or top 30 cryptocurrencies" isn't always useful.
Market capitalization alone doesn't tell you how well your orders will execute.
What matters more is the asset's actual liquidity, trading volume, spreads, order book depth, and derivatives market availability.
If your strategy focuses on small-cap altcoins or meme coins, it's worth checking how their trading costs and volatility fit within the Daily Loss Limit and Total Loss Limit before using that strategy during a prop Challenge.

A Practical Approach to Choosing Assets for a Prop Challenge
Just because dozens of markets are available doesn't mean you need to trade all of them.
The more assets you add to your watchlist, the harder it becomes to track market structure, news, and your total open risk.
One approach is to start with the most liquid assets and gradually expand your watchlist.
Starting With BTC
Bitcoin can be a practical primary market because of its high liquidity, substantial trading volume, and relatively tight spreads.
Focusing on one asset also simplifies your analysis.
You only need to track one market structure and how that market responds to trading sessions and news events.
However, there's no universal rule that says you should trade only BTC for your first 10–15 days.
If your strategy was originally developed and tested on ETH or another cryptocurrency, there's no reason to switch to Bitcoin just because you're taking a prop Challenge.
What matters is having a tested strategy and market conditions that support its execution.
Adding ETH
Ethereum can be a useful second market if it fits your strategy and offers a separate trading setup.
However, when trading BTC and ETH simultaneously, you need to account for correlation.
During major market or macroeconomic events, both assets can move in the same direction.
Before opening a second position, evaluate your combined exposure, not just the risk of each trade individually.
Adding Large-Cap Altcoins
SOL, BNB, and other liquid altcoins can also be added to your watchlist if they fit your trading strategy.
Before opening a position, check:
✓
Current liquidity and spreads
✓
Scheduled token unlocks
✓
Protocol upgrades and other major developments
✓
Correlation with your existing positions
✓
Total account risk
You shouldn't automatically reduce your position size just because you're trading an altcoin.
As with any other asset, position size should be based on your predefined risk, Stop Loss distance, and current market conditions.
The Correlation Trap
One common mistake when trading multiple cryptocurrencies is treating every position as completely independent.
Imagine opening three positions at the same time:
Long BTC + Long ETH + Long SOL
Technically, these are three separate trades.
But if all three assets respond to the same market conditions, they could move against you simultaneously.
In practice, three correlated positions can create one large directional exposure.
This is especially important ahead of FOMC meetings, CPI releases, NFP reports, and other events that can affect the entire crypto market at once.
When calculating position sizes, you need to consider both the risk of each individual trade and your total exposure across correlated positions.
For example, suppose you're risking 0.75% on each of two correlated long positions.
If both Stop Loss orders are triggered, your combined planned loss would be approximately 1.5% of your account, excluding fees and potential slippage.
That doesn't mean you should never hold three or more correlated positions.
What matters is understanding your total exposure and how simultaneous price movements could affect your Daily Loss Limit and Total Loss Limit.
