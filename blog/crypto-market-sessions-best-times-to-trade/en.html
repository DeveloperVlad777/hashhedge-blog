CRYPTO TRADING SESSIONS: WHEN TO TRADE AND WHEN TO STAY OUT OF THE MARKET
Crypto Trading Sessions: When to Trade and When to Stay Out of the Market
13 min read | October 3, 2026
The cryptocurrency market operates 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.
You can open or close a position at almost any time, but that does not mean trading conditions remain the same throughout the day.
Trading volume, liquidity, and volatility change depending on the time of day.
During some periods, the market may spend hours moving within a narrow range. During others, price can cover a significant distance in a short period of time.
That is why entry timing becomes another element of a trading system.
Understanding the characteristics of each session helps determine when the market typically becomes more active, where important intraday levels form, and which hours are worth monitoring more closely.

Times are shown in UTC. London and New York switch between daylight saving and standard time, so session hours shift by one hour. The U.S. and the UK change their clocks on different dates, which means session overlap times may differ during transition weeks.
Although crypto itself trades continuously, the day is generally divided into the Asian, London, and New York sessions, along with periods of increased activity when major sessions overlap.
This division reflects the activity of market participants from different regions and the operating hours of traditional financial markets. As a result, crypto trading volume and price behavior can change significantly throughout the day.
Asian Session: 00:00-09:00 UTC
The Asian session is often calmer than the European and U.S. sessions. Price may trade within a relatively narrow range, forming local highs and lows.
For a trader, these levels can be more important than the movement within the session itself. The high and low of the Asian range can become key reference points, with liquidity potentially resting beyond them.
Later, as market activity increases, price may test or sweep one of these levels.
The Asian session can therefore be used to prepare for the trading hours ahead:
✓
Identify the established range;
✓
Mark its high and low;
✓
Identify potential liquidity zones;
✓
Prepare scenarios for the European session.
Order blocks, FVGs, and other structural elements can also form during Asian trading hours.
However, the session time itself does not determine the quality of a setup, so it still needs to be evaluated within the broader market structure.
London Session: 07:00–16:00 UTC
As European trading begins, activity usually increases. For crypto traders, the period around the London open can be particularly interesting, as the market may transition from quieter trading into a more active move.
One scenario traders often watch involves the high and low of the Asian session.
Suppose BTC traded between $80,000 and $82,800 overnight. Once European activity begins, price may initially move beyond one side of the range, collect liquidity there, and then return inside.
For example: Asian range → move below $80,000 → liquidity sweep → return to the range → move higher.
This move may be considered a liquidity grab if the other characteristics also support this scenario.
The first hours of the London session are therefore often used to look for reactions to previously established levels. However, a move beyond the Asian high or low does not guarantee a reversal: price may continue moving and form a new trending impulse.
New York Session: 13:00–22:00 UTC
As the U.S. session begins, activity in the crypto market typically increases. Traders pay particular attention to the London–New York overlap, when participants from both the European and U.S. markets are active simultaneously.
From approximately 13:00 to 16:00 UTC, liquidity and volatility may increase significantly. During this period, price may test important intraday levels more frequently, sweep liquidity, and produce impulsive moves.
The U.S. session also overlaps with trading in the U.S. stock market. As a result, Bitcoin and other cryptocurrencies may be more strongly influenced by U.S. equity indices and macroeconomic events during these hours.
It is particularly important to consider the timing of major economic releases and Fed decisions. Inflation data, labor market reports, or interest rate decisions can sharply increase volatility within minutes and lead to wider spreads and slippage.
For traders, the New York session is particularly notable for the combination of high activity and a large number of significant events. However, higher volatility does not make every setup more reliable: market structure, liquidity, and risk still need to be considered before entering a trade.
Late New York and the Transition to the Asian Session: 22:00-00:00 UTC
After the main period of U.S. market activity, the market gradually transitions into a quieter period. Trading volumes may decline, and price movements may become less pronounced.
During these hours, price may continue trading within an already established range or test the high and low of the previous session. With lower activity, a breakout may fail to gain sufficient continuation and quickly return inside the range.
For this reason, the period between the end of the active New York session and the beginning of the Asian session is often used not to search for new trades, but to review the trading day and prepare levels for the next session.
If a setup does appear, however, it should not automatically be considered weak simply because of the time of day.
The session is additional context, not a standalone signal to enter or avoid a trade.
Weekly Structure: How Market Activity Changes
In addition to intraday sessions, crypto market activity also changes throughout the week. However, there is no strict rule stating that a particular day must be trending or quiet.
The day of the week is better used as additional context rather than as a standalone signal.
Monday. After the weekend, traditional financial market participants return. Price may continue the weekend move or begin forming a new weekly range. At the start of the week, it is therefore particularly important to wait for structure to develop rather than trying to predict the direction in advance.
Tuesday and Wednesday. By the middle of the week, the market has already received more information and liquidity, while the levels formed on Monday become reference points for further movement. Traders can monitor how price reacts to weekly highs, lows, and other significant levels.
Thursday. The market may continue the move established during the first half of the week or begin correcting it. Here, it is especially important to focus not on the day itself, but on where price is positioned relative to the established weekly range.
Friday. As the traditional trading week approaches its end, activity from some participants may decline, while positions may be closed or reduced ahead of the weekend. This can change the character of market movement.
On a Funded Account, Friday does not automatically require reducing position size or stopping trading at a specific time – the decision should depend on the trading system, current volatility, and established risk limits.
Weekends. Cryptocurrencies continue trading while most traditional markets are closed. Liquidity and volume on certain venues and instruments may decline, meaning that even a relatively small flow of orders can have a greater impact on price.
However, weekends do not necessarily mean the market will be quiet. Crypto news and events can trigger sharp moves.
Saturday and Sunday are therefore better viewed as a separate market regime, rather than automatically being considered days without trading opportunities.
Kill Zones: Periods of Increased Activity Within Trading Sessions
ICT concepts use the term Kill Zones to describe time windows within trading sessions when traders pay particularly close attention to the market.
The idea is not that a setup will necessarily work during these hours. Kill Zones help traders narrow down the time they spend monitoring charts and look for confirmation of an already established trading scenario during periods of increased activity.
Asian Kill Zone: 23:00–01:00 UTC
This is the beginning of Asian market activity. During this period, price may begin forming the move that will establish the boundaries of the subsequent Asian range. Compared with European and U.S. trading hours, the market often remains calmer.
London Kill Zone: 07:00–09:00 UTC
This period marks the beginning of active European trading. One scenario traders monitor is a move beyond the high or low of the Asian range, a liquidity sweep, and the subsequent price reaction.
New York Kill Zone: 13:00-15:00 UTC
This marks the beginning of U.S. market activity and also overlaps with the London session. Attention can shift to levels established earlier in the day: the London high and low, liquidity zones, FVGs, and other structural elements.
Second Half of New York: 19:00–21:00 UTC
Activity may differ from the first hours of the U.S. session, but the market can still test intraday extremes and form moves before transitioning into quieter trading hours.
In practice, Kill Zones can be used as a time filter. A trader identifies relevant levels and setups in advance, sets alerts, and checks the price reaction during the appropriate time window.
However, the start of a Kill Zone does not automatically increase the probability of a particular trade succeeding.
Time complements the analysis of structure, liquidity, and risk management – it does not replace them.

How Trading Sessions Affect Smart Money Setups
The timing of a setup can be used as additional context when analyzing Smart Money concepts. This is particularly relevant for liquidity grabs, order blocks, and FVGs.
However, the session itself does not automatically make a setup strong or weak. The priority is to evaluate market structure, price reaction, and confirmation, while using time as an additional filter.
Liquidity Grabs During Session Transitions
Traders often pay particular attention to transitions between major trading sessions, especially the London and New York opens.
By this point, the previous session has usually formed a local range. Stops and pending orders may be located above its high and below its low, making these levels potential targets for a liquidity grab.
For example, before the London open, a trader can mark the high and low of the Asian session and monitor how price reacts as it approaches these levels. A move beyond the range followed by a quick return inside can become part of a trading scenario if the broader structure confirms it.
Order Blocks and Their Formation Time
When analyzing an order block, it can be useful to consider not only its position on the chart but also the conditions under which it formed.
If an order block appeared before a strong impulsive move during a period of high market activity, that context may be more interesting than a block formed during a quiet consolidation.
However, the quality of an order block should not be judged based on the trading session alone. It is more important to evaluate what movement followed its formation, whether a break of structure occurred, and whether the zone remains relevant when price returns to it.
FVGs and Imbalances Within Trading Sessions
A similar logic applies to Fair Value Gaps (FVGs).
During a strong impulse, price may move rapidly through a range and leave an imbalance behind.
If such a move is accompanied by a structural change and occurs during a period of active trading, an FVG can be considered a potential reaction area when price later returns.
However, not every price gap formed during a Kill Zone automatically becomes a significant FVG. The context of the move should be evaluated first, followed by the timing of its formation.
Managing Trading Sessions on a Funded Account
Round-the-clock trading creates an additional temptation to constantly search for new entries. After several hours without a trade, it can become easy to lower your setup requirements simply because the market remains open.
On a Funded Account, this approach is particularly risky: a loss counts the same regardless of what time the position was opened. That is why predefined trading hours can become part of risk management.
Define Your Active Trading Hours
Instead of constantly monitoring the market, you can select specific periods in which your strategy has historically produced the most consistent results.
For example, a trader may focus on the London open, the London–New York overlap, or only the U.S. session. The specific window depends on the strategy, traded instruments, and the trader's own time zone.
The key is not to choose a time simply because a particular session is considered "the best." Optimal trading hours should be determined based on your own trading statistics.
This approach both limits the number of impulsive entries and simplifies the trading process: outside the selected window, there is no need to constantly search for another position to open.
Holding Positions Over the Weekend on a Funded Account
The crypto market does not close on weekends, so technically, a position can continue to be held.
However, market conditions on Saturday and Sunday may differ from weekdays due to changes in trading volume and participant activity.
Before holding a position over the weekend, it is worth assessing:
✓
Remaining buffer to the Daily Loss Limit and Total Loss Limit;
✓
Open position size;
✓
Stop Loss placement;
✓
Current volatility;
✓
Potential news risks.
There is no need to automatically close all positions at a specific time on Friday. The decision should depend on the trading system and acceptable risk.
